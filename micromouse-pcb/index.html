FEATURED HARDWARE PROJECT

Micromouse PCB & Electrical System

Designed and tested a custom PCB for an autonomous Micromouse robot, replacing a breadboard-based electrical system with a compact, integrated platform for sensing, motor control, and power distribution.

OVERVIEW

Building the electrical system into the robot itself

Earlier Union College Micromouse robots relied on breadboards, loose wiring, and larger stacked components. This made the robots heavier, harder to troubleshoot, and more susceptible to electrical connection problems during motion.

I designed a custom PCB that served as both the electrical platform and part of the robot's mechanical structure. The goal was to reduce wiring, lower the overall size and weight, improve reliability, and make future debugging easier.

SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE

Sensors, motor feedback, and control

The system uses six IR distance sensors positioned around the front of the robot to measure nearby maze walls. Two motor encoders provide feedback on motor speed and direction.

The microcontroller receives the sensor and encoder inputs and commands the motor controller, which supplies the current required by the two drive motors.

Electrical schematic connecting the microcontroller, motor controller, IR sensors, motor encoders, switches, and power input.

DESIGN PROCESS

Designing around electrical and mechanical constraints

01

Component Placement

Positioned six IR sensors around the front of the PCB while balancing sensor range, routing space, motor placement, and the board's physical size.

02

Motor & Weight Placement

Moved the motors toward the center of the PCB in later revisions to improve balance and shift the robot's axis of rotation closer to its center.

03

Power & Ground Planes

Reworked the original four-layer approach by introducing power and ground planes, allowing the final PCB to use a simpler two-layer architecture.

04

Trace & Clearance Design

Considered component current requirements, trace widths, conductor spacing, routing density, and manufacturing constraints while developing the board.

ITERATION

Three PCB revisions

The project went through three PCB iterations. The second revision exposed several issues, including a missing motor connection, shorted encoder pins, insufficient clearance for the motor mounts, and incomplete silkscreen markings.

I used those failures to revise the design before manufacturing the third version. The final revision corrected the electrical connections, improved mechanical clearances, and added clearer silkscreen markings to make assembly and troubleshooting easier.

TESTING & VALIDATION

Validating the design before and after assembly

Design Rule Check

Used PCB design-rule checks before fabrication to identify violations involving routing, spacing, and electrical connections.

Continuity Testing

Cross-referenced PCB connections against the schematic and used a digital multimeter to verify continuity before and after soldering.

IR Sensor Testing

Tested each sensor against maze walls and plotted analog responses to verify changes in distance were being transmitted correctly.

Motor Encoder Testing

Monitored encoder feedback while varying motor speed and used oscilloscope-based troubleshooting for motor and encoder signals.

RESULT

A smaller, cleaner, and more reliable Micromouse platform

The final PCB passed continuity testing and successfully supported the IR sensors, motor encoders, motor controller, and microcontroller.

Moving from loose breadboard wiring to an integrated PCB reduced wiring clutter, improved debugging access, lowered the physical profile of the robot, and created a platform that could continue to be used by future members of the robotics team.
